When your child is struggling with big feelings, behaviour that feels overwhelming, or changes that are hard to put into words, it can be hard to know what kind of support will actually help. **Child-Centered Play Therapy** is a gentle approach that meets your child exactly where they are — through play.

## A space where your child leads

In Child-Centered Play Therapy, your child is not asked to talk through their feelings or sit still while an adult guides the conversation. Instead, the playroom becomes a safe, accepting space where they are free to lead.

The therapist provides a carefully selected range of play materials — sand, figurines, dolls, drawing tools, puppets, and more — alongside the therapeutic boundaries that make the space feel safe. From there, your child chooses what to play with, how to play, and for how long. There is no right or wrong way.

Within this held relationship, children begin to express what they are carrying — sometimes through words, but more often through play.

## What happens in a session

Sessions are typically once a week, in a calm and predictable environment. The therapist stays consistent, attentive, and emotionally present — following your child's lead, reflecting what is noticed, and trusting the process.

This isn't about teaching coping skills or behaviour strategies. It's about creating the conditions where your child feels safe enough to process difficult experiences, regulate their emotions, and grow in their own way and at their own pace.

## Who it can help

Child-Centered Play Therapy is well-suited for young children who are experiencing:

- Big emotions, frequent meltdowns, or emotional overwhelm
- Behavioural challenges or sudden changes in behaviour
- Anxiety, withdrawal, or low confidence
- Difficulty adjusting to life changes — a new sibling, parental separation, moving, starting school
- Loss, grief, or other difficult experiences

It's not a quick fix. Research consistently shows meaningful change takes time — the work unfolds over many sessions, depending on your child's needs and circumstances.

## What it supports over time

This approach gently fosters:

- **Emotional expression** — finding ways to put feelings into play rather than behaviour
- **Self-regulation** — managing big feelings with growing confidence
- **Self-understanding** — making sense of inner experiences
- **Resilience** — a deeper trust in their own capacity to navigate life's challenges

## A note on parents

While your child attends sessions, you remain central to the work. I keep you informed of how things are going, and we meet periodically (usually every 5 or 6 child sessions) to reflect together. The most meaningful change happens when home and therapy are connected.
