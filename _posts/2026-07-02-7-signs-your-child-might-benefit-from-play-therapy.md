Play Therapy can be helpful for children and families navigating a wide range of challenges. While every child is unique, here are seven signs that play therapy might be worth exploring for your child.

## Difficulty Managing Big Emotions

Does your child have intense tantrums, frequent meltdowns, or seem overwhelmed by emotions that feel too big to handle? Play Therapy gives children tools to understand and regulate their emotional world.

## Behavioural Challenges

Aggression, defiance, withdrawal, or difficulty following routines — these behaviours often mask unmet needs or unprocessed feelings. Play Therapy addresses the root, not just the behaviour.

## Difficulty with Transitions or Life Changes

A new sibling, a move, parental separation, starting school, or a loss — these events can stir up big feelings that children struggle to put into words. Play Therapy provides a contained space to process these experiences.

## Withdrawal or Sudden Changes

If your child has become unusually quiet, anxious, or has lost interest in things they used to enjoy, this may be a sign they are carrying something heavy. Play Therapy gives these feelings a place to surface safely.

## Struggles with Confidence or Self-Worth

Children who frequently say things like "I can't do it" or who seem to give up easily may be carrying deep self-doubt. Play Therapy can help rebuild a sense of capability and inner strength.

## Difficulty in Relationships with Peers or Family

Whether it's conflict with siblings, isolation from peers, or tension at home, Play Therapy can help children understand and express what they're experiencing in relationship with others.

## Experiencing or Recovering from Trauma

Traumatic experiences — including accidents, loss, abuse, or witnessing distressing events — can leave lasting imprints. Play Therapy is a gentle, evidence-based way to support recovery.
